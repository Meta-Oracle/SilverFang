# SILVER FANG — Story Bible
*"Monsters need myths. Myths need money."*

## The World — A.X. 2347

**A.X.** — *Anno Exhaustionis*, the Years of Depletion. The calendar reset in
**A.X. 2333**, the year the last workable veins died: lithium, cobalt, the
rare earths, even deep-crust thermal water. Fourteen years ago. Every fiat
currency on the planet was backed, in the end, by the promise that more could
always be dug up. When that promise died, money died with it — in about six
weeks.

Into the gap stepped **Scematica Dynamics**, a mid-tier genomics conglomerate
that had spent the previous decade quietly buying gene patents nobody wanted.
Their pitch was elegant: the only resource still being *created* on a dead
planet is information — specifically, **engineered genetic code**. A novel,
functional, patented genome is provably scarce, provably unique, and provably
expensive to produce.

So they minted against it.

**SCEMA** — the Scematica token — is a proof-of-scarcity currency where every
unit is collateralized by sequenced, patented genome strings held in the
company's vaults. The world laughed for a month, starved for a year, and then
adopted it. Today SCEMA is the only money that matters from the Atlantic
Sprawl to the Pacific Char. Wages, rent, water rations, funerals: all SCEMA.

The city is **Argent City**, built on the bones of three older ones, named for
the silver-gray smog that never lifts. Scematica's headquarters — **the
Mint** — is the only building tall enough to see the sun.

## The Abominations

Genetic splicing and cybernetic augmentation were the technologies that were
supposed to save the world, and in A.X. 2333 the safeties came off both. The
official history: desperate black-lab splicing during the collapse produced
self-replicating chimeric organisms that escaped containment. The city calls
them by the old names — werewolves, chimeras, reapers — because the old names
travel faster than taxonomy.

The truth (the player's discovery): **the outbreaks are scheduled.**

Inside Scematica they are not called monsters. They are called **yields**.
Every engineered organism is a new patented genome, and every new patented
genome mints new SCEMA. The creatures are walking collateral. The company
breeds them, brands them with mythology (the Myth Department is a real
department), releases them on a quarterly schedule into pre-selected
districts, and then pays bounty hunters — in SCEMA — to destroy them.

The loop is the entire economy:

1. **Mint** — a new genome is sequenced and patented; SCEMA is issued against it.
2. **Release** — the organism is released; fear drives SCEMA demand (bounty
   insurance, wall tax, augment leases).
3. **Burn** — a Gun-Hunter destroys the organism. The patent becomes a
   *closed string* — provably unique forever, never reproducible. Scarcity
   value rises. The bounty paid out is a fraction of the appreciation.
4. Repeat.

Monsters are not a side effect of the economy. **Monsters are monetary
policy.**

## Gun-Hunters

The **Gun-Hunters** are Scematica's licensed cull force — Asset Recovery
Division, known on the street as **the Kennel**. They are celebrities, folk
heroes, and indentured servants all at once:

- Every hunter's combat augments are **leased**, not owned. The lease is
  denominated in SCEMA and compounds.
- Clause 9 of the standard contract: *"Organic and inorganic collateral
  reverts to the Issuer upon default."* Default on your augment lease and the
  company owns your body. Most hunters die before they notice they were
  always going to.
- Bounties pay well enough that no hunter ever quite escapes the math.

## Cast

**SILVER** — the player. Kennel designation S-1L (the street read it as
"Silver" and it stuck). The best cull record in Argent City and no memory of
volunteering for the program that built him. His augments take to chimeric
tissue *abnormally well* — the company calls it compatibility; the truth is
ancestry. Silver's augment line was derived from **K-9 Protocol**, the same
splice that produced the werewolf yields. The **Awakened** state isn't
technology overclocking. It's the splice *expressing*. Silver is hunting his
own family tree, and his employer holds the patent on his blood.

Voice: terse, dry, asks the question everyone else has agreed not to ask.

**VESPER** — Silver's dispatch handler. Calm, corporate-polite, unfailingly
precise, with an odd habit of saying "we" about events from before she was
assigned. Reveal: Vesper is not a person. She is a **neural compile** of
Hunter E-V3 "Evester" — a Kennel legend who died nine years ago, three days
after asking the wrong question about bounty timestamps. The company kept her
voice because hunters trust it. Somewhere inside the compile, Evester is
still asking her question.

Voice: clipped, warm-but-wrong, says terrible things in scheduling language.

**DR. LIORA SENN** — Hemotype R&D, the geneticist who designed the
genome-collateral binding, i.e. the person who made monsters mintable. Now
scattering encrypted **datashards** across cull zones like breadcrumbs,
addressed to whichever hunter lives long enough to read them all. She cannot
go public — she is the collateral architect; confession is suicide. Her plan
is colder: give a hunter the whole picture *in the company's own documents*,
and let the company's best weapon decide what it was built for.

Voice: precise, guilt-eaten, mordant. Writes like a scientist who has started
talking to the dead.

**CHAIRMAN AUREL VOSS** — founder of Scematica Dynamics, author of the SCEMA
white paper, the man who decided the world. Appears in the demo only through
directives and one final voice line. Voss does not consider himself a villain;
he considers himself the only adult in the room. The Depletion was coming
regardless — he merely made sure it arrived *on schedule, with a successor
currency ready*. (Datashard 07: some of the vein collapses were demolition
charges.)

Voice: serene, reasonable, the most frightening person in the story.

## The Reveal Ladder

The conspiracy unfolds in three interlocking strands — **money** (the loop),
**body** (Silver's origin), **handler** (Vesper) — so that every answer opens
a different question. No single beat says "the company did it"; the player
assembles it.

| # | Source | Strand | Fact learned |
|---|--------|--------|--------------|
| 1 | Dialogue, Encounter 1 clear | money | Bounty was *posted* hours before the outbreak was *reported* |
| 2 | Shard 01 — Payroll Anomaly | money | It's systematic: 41 of 41 bounties predate their outbreaks |
| 3 | Shard 02 — Myth Dept. memo | money | Creatures are branded internally before release ("yields") |
| 4 | Shard 03 — Patent String | money | Chimera gene-tags are SCEMA mint hashes; genome = collateral |
| 5 | Shard 04 — Lease, Clause 9 | body | Hunters' bodies are repossessable collateral |
| 6 | Dialogue, Encounter 2 clear | handler | Vesper "remembers" a cull from before her assignment |
| 7 | Shard 05 — K-9 Postmortem | body | Werewolf line came from hunter volunteers; "Subject S-1L: expression latent" |
| 8 | Shard 06 — Vesper fragment | handler | Dispatch logs in first person from a hunter dead nine years |
| 9 | Shard 07 — Depletion audit | money | The Depletion itself was partly staged |
| 10 | Shard 08 — Release Schedule | money | Next outbreak is scheduled; "casualty budget" attached |
| 11 | Shard 09 — Senn's confession | all | The architect speaks; "follow the silver thread" |
| 12 | Shard 10 — Voss directive | endgame | Awakened assets flagged for recall. Silver is on the list. |

Rule of thumb for the writing: **Vesper schedules, Senn confesses, Voss
explains, Silver asks.** The company never lies — every document is true,
which is what makes it unbearable.

## Style Notes

- Currency is diegetic everywhere: bounties, augment leases, codex entries
  about monetary policy. The SCEMA counter on the HUD *is* a story element —
  every kill paying out is the loop, visible, all game long.
- Nobody says "evil corporation." The horror is bookkeeping.
- The old myth names (werewolf, chimera) are always in quotes in company
  documents and never in quotes on the street.

# SILVER FANG — THE DARK MOON ARC

*The fourth and final movement. Begins after the Scematica arc has fully
resolved — the currency saved, the Apium Gestalt broken, Dark Fang ended, the
two continua bled into one. The survivors think the war is over. They have only
finished the chapter that someone else was always reading over their shoulder.*

---

## I. THE AGENCY OF CHAOS

There is a timeline older than all the others — older in the sense that matters,
which is not age but *position*. It sits further back along the river. Its year
is 2026, and it is, to the smallest headline and the dullest Tuesday, our world.
The same wars. The same markets. The same quiet anxieties scrolling past at two
in the morning. If you lived there you would never notice the difference,
because the difference is the one thing built specifically so that you cannot.

Every event of consequence in that world — every coronation and every crash,
every miracle drug and every martyred prophet — was *arranged*. Not all at once,
and never crudely. Arranged the way a gardener arranges a hedge: a cut here, a
season of patience, the whole shape only visible from a height no citizen is
ever allowed to stand at. The hands that did the cutting called themselves, at
first, the Illuminati. They were real. They were bankers and cardinals and
natural philosophers who met in Renaissance back rooms and decided, with the
serene arrogance of men who have never once been told *no*, that history was too
important to be left to the people living through it.

For five centuries the lineage held. It changed names the way a snake changes
skin. And then it came to the year the Maya had circled.

**2012.** The end of the Long Count. The date a dying calendar handed down as
the close of an age — which the panicked read as the end of the world, and which
the cabal read correctly, as the end of *a cycle*. So on that symbolic threshold
they shed the last of the old names and reformed, openly to themselves and
invisibly to everyone else, as the **Agency of Chaos — A.o.C.** Technocrats.
Sovereign-wealth ghosts. The handful of high-society founders whose portraits
hang in no public hall. They took the apocalypse as a christening. *The old
world ended on schedule,* they told one another, lifting their glasses. *We are
what comes after.*

They were never wrong about the prophecy. Only about which world it meant.

---

## II. SIX

The **Black Moon Six Clan** is a clan of one — for now. Its first and founding
member is the woman the world will eventually learn to fear under a single
syllable: **Six.**

She is young, still in her twenties, and she has spent nearly all of those years
inside the Agency that she now intends to burn down from the inside. The clan
she founded will one day be drawn entirely from women, each a different facet of
the discipline — the silencer, the poisoner, the blade, the shadow, the talker,
the one who is simply never seen — so that between them the Black Moon Six can
answer any shape a fight might take. But that is the future. Six is the seed of
it, and she carries all those facets alone until there are sisters to carry them
with her.

Her style did not begin with the Agency. It began with her **great-grandfather**
— a master in the old severe lineage, the kind of teacher the legends sketch as
*Pai Mei*: cruel, exact, decades deep in a martial art entirely his own. He
trained Six from the time she could stand. He had no use for shortcuts, and he
had, locked away, a thing that was nothing but shortcut: a **scroll** of hidden
ninpo and ninjutsu, techniques that bent the rules he had spent a lifetime
mastering. He never used it. His own art was already complete; the scroll, to
him, was a temptation he had simply outlived.

When Six was **sixteen**, he gave it to her. Not as a reward. As a warning, and
a wager. *I never needed this,* he told her. *You will. Learn what is in it, and
learn what it costs, because everything in it costs.* Then he taught her one
last form and did not teach her again.

She read the scroll the way she does everything: completely, secretly, and with
a patience that frightens people who notice it. The ninpo in it is the spine of
the techniques the Agency has never catalogued, because she has never once shown
them the real ones.

---

## III. THE SABOTAGE

Six does not hate the Agency the way a revolutionary hates a regime. She hates it
the way a surgeon hates a tumour — clinically, and with a plan. For years she has
been the perfect operative: flawless, decorated, trusted at the founding board's
own table. And for every operation she has run *for* them, she has run a second,
smaller one *against* them — a corrupted ledger, a misrouted asset, a founder
quietly turned, a truth left where exactly one other person would find it.

By the time the Dark Moon arc opens she is close. Closer than the Agency has any
idea. One more move and the whole five-century hedge comes apart at the root.

What she does not yet know — what none of them know — is that her timeline is not
sealed. The convergence that ended the Scematica war did not simply weld two
continua together; it cracked the wall between *all* of them. And through that
crack, the Agency of Chaos has felt, for the first time in five hundred years,
something it cannot arrange: **other worlds.** Silver. Hilo. Lucas Vegas. Powers
that were never on any of their boards. The A.o.C's instinct is the only one it
has ever had — to orchestrate them, to fold these timelines into the hedge.

Six's instinct is the opposite. If her sabotage is going to land, she needs them.
And if their fight is going to mean anything past the Scematica's ashes, they
need the one person alive who has spent her whole life learning exactly how the
hand behind history actually moves.

---

## IV. THE DATE, KEPT

Here is the symbol the founders toasted on, come back for them.

The Maya never said the world would end. They said a cycle would. The Agency was
*born* on that date — which makes the Agency, by its own chosen christening, a
cycle. And cycles, the calendar insists, **close.** Every move A.o.C made to
prove they were what comes after the end only bound them tighter to an ending
they wrote into their own founding myth. Six is not breaking a prophecy when she
finishes them. She is keeping one. The apocalypse the Maya marked was never
civilization's. It was always the quiet, exact, inevitable fall of the hand that
thought it had bought the future outright — undone, in the end, by the one
ninja it raised, trusted, and never once truly saw.

The Dark Moon rises on the day the Long Count runs out for real.

---

## V. THE MEASURE OF A MASTER

She was sixteen when the scroll changed hands, and she did not stop. Twelve years
of it — the old man's complete and merciless art layered under the scroll's
hidden ninpo, every form drilled in secret while she rose through an Agency that
believed it was watching her closely. By the time the convergence cracks her
world open she is **twenty-eight**: young enough to still be underestimated, old
enough that the underestimation is a fatal mistake. A full master. The first true
adept the Black Moon Six lineage has ever produced, because she is the one who
made the lineage.

## VI. THE SEVENTEENTH

The Agency of Chaos is run by founders — twelve of them at the start, the cabal's
old number. By the time of the Dark Moon arc that inner board has grown to
**sixteen**. Six is the **seventeenth.** The newest. The youngest ever inducted.
She earned the chair the only way the Agency respects: by completing mission after
impossible mission until refusing her a seat would have been an insult to their
own scoreboard. They handed the wolf the keys because the wolf had the best
numbers.

A seat that high comes with a vault that deep. Six has access to technology far
past her timeline's 2026 — Agency materiel, prototypes, the toys a five-century
conspiracy hoards. But that is only half her arsenal. The other half does not
belong to the Agency at all. It belongs to **him.**

## VII. THE ONE MAN IN THE BLACK MOON

The clan's first and most carefully kept secret is this: the Black Moon Six is
*not* only women. There is exactly one man in it — and he is, blow for blow, the
equal of Six herself.

His name is **Sterling.** He has the iconic silver hair of his blood, but where
his father's is short and brutal, Sterling's is long, straight, and flowing, worn
loose like a banner; a neat pointed goatee and a thin moustache, silver as the
rest. He is a strong-and-silent man — a deep gentlemanly calm, rarely a word, a
warmth that does not announce itself. He fights with a single absurdly long
blade, a katana enhanced past metallurgy: it ripples the field around its edge so
it cuts *anything* — a **gravity-ripper katana**, parting tank armour and bedrock
with the same unhurried stroke.

He is **Silver's son** — from a timeline all his own.

The second secret, the one Six keeps even from her own sisters: Sterling is her
**partner.** Her boyfriend. For most of her life Six despised men — a clean,
practiced contempt, armour worn so long she'd forgotten it was armour. Then she
met Sterling, and his patience and his unhurried, genuinely *kind* smile did what
no argument ever could: it melted a heart she'd spent twenty years calcifying.
She would deny it to anyone. She would die before the clan learned it. It is, to
her, the single softest and most dangerous thing she owns.

## VIII. THE WORLD THAT WON AND DIED ANYWAY

Sterling's timeline is a splinter — separate from every other thread, yet a near
mirror of the one Future-Silver came from, with a single fatal difference. In
*his* world, his father **won.** Silver broke the Scematica Division clean. The
currency held. Humanity, for one bright generation, was free.

And the Agency of Chaos was watching.

A.o.C exists in that thread too, older and quieter than Scematica ever was — and
older than Scematica's god, too. The Agency was *present at the creation of the
AI that would become the Apium Gestalt.* They were in the room, as they are in
every room that matters, when the seed of the hive first compiled. Scematica
Division, the Gestalt, the whole five-century-spanning war Silver fought and won
— all of it grew inside a garden the Agency of Chaos had already planted. Apium
thought itself the apex intelligence; it was a *crop.* So when Silver's world
broke free, the Agency let the whole war play out untouched — because it had
watched that AI come into being, it understood exactly what had just been beaten,
and it wanted to *see it.* It wanted to know whether a humanity that threw off
even a god of the Agency's own quiet making could be permitted to exist. It studied Silver's victory the way it studies everything: coldly,
completely. Then it decided the answer was no. A free world it had not authored
was an error in the hedge. So the Agency moved in — not with monsters, with the
patient overwhelming weight of five centuries of orchestration — and **globally
dominated and erased** the only timeline where Silver ever truly won, folding its
stolen victory into the cabal's ledger like a closed account.

Sterling escaped through a **dimensional time-machine** — one device out of an
arsenal of future tech he carries still, the inheritance of a murdered world. He
went looking for the hand that killed his home and found it wearing a familiar
face: an Agency of Chaos, in a timeline where it could still be stopped. And
inside that Agency, already at war with it, already seventeenth at its table, he
found Six.

The son of the man who won, and the woman who never stopped fighting — both armed
with the future, both hunting the same five-hundred-year-old hand. The Dark Moon
rises with two blades, not one.

## IX. LYRA, AND THE APEX

Sterling's mother is **Lyra Fang**, and she is no soldier's wife. She is one of
the most powerful beings her timeline ever produced — Silver's equal in a way no
enemy ever was. Where Silver's gift is the overcharged psychic-kinetic storm of
his awakened form, Lyra's is something the multiverse has seen in no one else:
**gravitic-spatial dominion.** She does not throw force; she rewrites the rule
that force obeys. She opens crushing wells and weightless voids, anchors a
charging titan to the floor like a pinned moth, *folds* the distance between two
points so a step crosses a battlefield, hangs miniature event horizons in the air
that swallow blades and bullets whole. Gravity is a law to everyone else. To Lyra
it is a suggestion.

When Silver and Lyra conceived their son, both were at the absolute summit of
their power — two apex beings at their peak, in the same moment. What they made
was not the average of them. It was **beyond** them. Sterling carries **APEX
genetics**: Silver's awakened psychic-kinetic surge *and* Lyra's gravitic-spatial
dominion, fused and inherited whole — at a level higher than either parent ever
reached, and at birth, *raw, untrained, natural.* His gravity-ripper katana is
not a gift of technology so much as a focusing lens for his mother's blood; the
blue overcharge that ghosts his strikes is his father's. Before he ever trained a
day, he was already more than the two strongest people in his world. Then his
father trained him anyway.

## X. THE QUIET DEATH

Here is how the Agency killed a god.

When Silver broke the Scematica Division, he understood something the celebrations
did not: a victory that loud, that total, draws **eyes** — high-level dark
entities that take notice of any mortal who topples a power of that order. So
Silver did the one thing no enemy expected of the man who never stopped fighting.
He **stopped.** He hid that he had a wife. He hid that he had a son. He buried the
fact of his own happiness so deep that not even the watching dark could find a
family to threaten, and he retired into a quiet, guarded, invisible life.

It worked against everything — except the one watcher that had been there the
whole time. The Agency of Chaos was *already aware* of Silver, of Lyra, of the
boy. His secrecy hid him from the cosmos; it did not hide him from the gardeners
who had been tending his timeline since before he was born. And so they waited
until the warrior had finally, fatally, lowered his guard — until he was a retired
husband and not a standing god — and *that*, the open throat of a happy man who
thought he was finally safe, is the only reason the Agency of Chaos was ever able
to kill him at all.

## XI. THE ONE-MAN WAR

Sterling did not know any of it at first. He knew only that his father was dead in
a way that did not add up. So he went looking — not for revenge, at first, but for
the *answer to a question:* who was actually at play in his father's death?

The question led him to the Agency. And the Agency turned a grieving son into a
**one-man war.** It began as an investigation and became a campaign that spanned
every global zone of his world — Sterling, alone, with his mother's gravity in his
blade and his father's storm in his hands and the inheritance of a murdered future
in his pack, tearing the Agency of Chaos apart cell by cell until, at the end,
he had *single-handedly* beaten it. One man. Every zone. The whole hand, broken,
by the boy it never accounted for.

And then he crossed over. He came to *this* Silver's timeline carrying a secret
he has shared with no one — not the convergence, not even Six. He came to destroy
the Agency of Chaos here too, yes.

But that is not the only reason he came.

## XII. THE SECRET, AND THE SILENCE

In Sterling's timeline, he and Six were never lovers. They were *best friends* —
the closest two people he had — and then she died. The Agency took her the way it
took everyone he loved. And in the aftermath, going through what she left behind,
he found a message she had written and sealed *to be opened only in the event of
her death:* a confession. All the years of feeling she had never once said aloud,
left where he would only ever read it if it was already too late.

He lost his best friend and discovered she had loved him in the same moment, from
the same envelope. The two wounds arrived together and fused into one. That is the
thing that broke his voice. The strong, gentle silence the clan reads as mystique
is grief — Sterling went **nonverbal** the day he read a dead woman's heart and
realized he could never answer it.

So he crossed the dimensional seam for two reasons, and only ever admits the
first. He came to destroy the Agency of Chaos. And he came to **save Six** — to
reach a living version of the friend he buried, before this timeline's Agency
could write the same ending twice. In *this* thread he did the one thing he never
got to do in his own: he stayed close, he reciprocated, he let the warmth he'd
been too late for the first time actually land. The romance the present-day Six
guards so fiercely is, to Sterling, a second chance he tore a hole in the
multiverse to get.

## XIII. THE LONG CON

Six was inducted into the Agency at **eighteen** — two years after the scroll,
already a prodigy, already playing a game no one at that table knew had started.
For years she did exactly what an ambitious operative should: she gained their
trust by completing missions of escalating, impossible difficulty until the board
saw her as indispensable.

Then she met Sterling, and the game changed shape. With access to his arsenal of
genuine future technology, Six began feeding the Agency *the wrong half of it* —
**obsolete, outdated** future tech, carefully chosen junk dressed as treasure. It
did three things at once. It **stalled** them, sending their best minds down years
of dead-end reverse-engineering. It made Six look like the most **invaluable
asset** the Agency had ever recruited. And it convinced the founders that, armed
with this "impossible" technology, they had finally reached the **final stage of
global destabilization** — that they were, at last, ahead of the curve.

They were a full era behind it. While their labs reverse-engineered her garbage,
Six was quietly building the *real* thing: superior weaponry, military vehicles,
and armour drawn from the actually-advanced arsenal — an arming of the Black Moon
Six that the Agency cannot see and could not match. She is, at every moment,
twenty steps ahead.

And the trap is already set. The obsolete tech she gifted them is now fully
**integrated into the entire A.o.C intelligence-network infrastructure** — and
woven through it, dormant, is a **total shutdown-and-revert protocol.** On her
command, the very technology that made the Agency feel invincible goes dark and
rolls back, blinding the five-century hand at the exact moment the Black Moon Six
strikes.

She can do all of this because intelligence is only the first of her secret
talents — a mind operating at a **superhuman tier, possibly among the highest the
human species has ever produced** — layered over the hidden ninpo of the scroll
and an old master's complete art. The Agency recruited a genius. It never grasped
that the genius had been the one recruiting *it.*

## XIV. THE SEVEN

So the Dark Moon arc converges on a single operation: the **Black Moon Six secret
weapon assault**, every member committed at once. Seven blades — the **six women**
of the clan, each a different facet of the discipline, and **Sterling**, the
seventh and the secret, Silver's apex-born son. Six springs the shutdown protocol;
the Agency's stolen edge dies in its hands; and the clan she built, armed with the
real future, falls on a blind five-century empire all at the same instant.

The Long Count runs out for real — on the Agency's own christening date, by the
hand of the operative it raised, trusted, and never once truly saw.

## XV. THE TIMELINE, THE TALENTS, THE BLADE

*(On the name: the clan is formally the **Black Moon Six** — known in the field,
and through this era, as the **Dark Moon Six.** Same clan, same six-and-one.)*

**The order of it.** Six received the scroll at **sixteen.** She was inducted
into the Agency at **eighteen.** And **shortly after that induction** — trust
barely earned, game barely begun — she founded the Black Moon Six. The clan came
first; the sisters first; *then* Sterling. His arrival and debut fall **after**
the founding, the seventh blade joining a clan that already existed, not the
reason it was built. The con with the obsolete future-tech only takes its final
shape once he and his real arsenal are at her side.

**The talents she has shown no one.** The Agency thinks it recruited a brilliant
operative. It has no idea what it actually has — because Six has kept the true
extent of herself secret from everyone alive *except* the members of the Black
Moon Six, past and present (and some of those are gone now, taken on the road to
this moment). Beneath the public record runs:

- a **superhuman intellect** — reckoned among the **top three minds of her entire
  timeline**;
- **superhuman swordsmanship**, married to her great-grandfather's complete art;
- **summoning jutsu** and **substitution jutsu**, real techniques from the scroll
  that the Agency has never once seen her use;
- **ki-based ninpo** — she projects and *extends* her own ki and lands it through
  her fists, fusing her raw physical strength with ki-strength and ki-damage so
  that a single punch hits like a siege engine wrapped in a soul;
- and **vastly superhuman physical strength**, enough to swing what should be an
  immovable blade like a reed — fast enough that, in full flurry, the strokes
  blur into a **hyper gravity-ripper vortex**, a whirling storm of ripped-space
  techniques spun out of nothing but her speed and that blade.

**The blade.** Her arsenal is a full kit of future-tech *ninja* gear — projectiles
and tools that take the old shinobi ideas and rebuild them out of tomorrow:
homing micro-shuriken drones, programmable smoke, gravity-tipped kunai. Her
signature weapon's base form is a long, elegant **extended katana** (longer than
any common sword, though never quite as long as Sterling's), its flat inscribed
with a Japanese ninjutsu incantation. In that form it carries the same
**gravity-ripper** technology her partner's blade does, powered by the same
impossible fuel: **stabilized Element 115** — the gravitic heart of every ripper
in the convergence, the substance the Agency of Chaos has chased through five
centuries of myth and never once been allowed to truly hold.

But the incantation lets the weapon **change form** on command — and its true
shape is her **ultimate secret technique.** Spoken in full, the katana unfolds
into a massive, devastating **two-handed greatsword** that *overdrives* the
gravity-rip past cutting matter and begins **tearing holes in the fabric of the
universe itself** — wounds in spacetime opened by a swing. Married to her flurry,
that is the **hyper gravity-ripper vortex** at its apex: a whirlwind of a strength
that should not exist, a speed that should not be possible, and a blade that
unmakes the space it moves through. The genius the Agency recruited has been
forging the future out of the one element they wanted most — and the finished
weapon does not just cut them. It deletes the room they are standing in.

---

*Six joins the roster as the fourth and final playable hero: founder of the Black
Moon Six Clan, last student of a Pai-Mei lineage, keeper of a scroll of hidden
ninpo, the seventeenth seat of the Agency of Chaos turned against it — and the
only fighter in the convergence who has been at war with the people behind the
curtain since before she was old enough to be told what the curtain was. At her
side, kept secret from her own sisters, is Sterling: Silver's son, sole man of
the Black Moon Six, gravity-ripper in hand, the last survivor of the one world
where his father won.*
